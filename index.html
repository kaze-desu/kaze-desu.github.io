About

I am an MSc student in Artificial Intelligence at the University of Macau (2026–2028), advised by Prof. Zhedong Zheng in the AIGC-DL Lab. My research sits at the intersection of multimodal large language models, spatial intelligence, and agentic systems. I am interested in how MLLM agents reason about the physical world, verify fine-grained visual evidence, and act on it.

Before that, I spent two years as a research intern at MAIS Lab, Institute of Automation, Chinese Academy of Sciences (CASIA), advised by Dr. Ajian Liu and Prof. Yanyan Liang, where I worked on fine-grained MLLM verification (LocalAgent, NeurIPS 2026) and flexible-modal face anti-spoofing (FM-MoE, IEEE T-IFS 2026). I received my BSc in Software Engineering (Artificial Intelligence track) from Macau University of Science and Technology in 2026.

Currently, I focus on the underlying visual and spatial capabilities of pretrained multimodal models. In particular, I study how a model can infer 3D spatial relations, estimate object scale and position consistently across viewpoints, and turn that visual understanding into plans and actions that further improve perception. I also explore how agentic designs with sequential execution, interaction memory, and broader permissions to act can elicit and reuse pretrained knowledge across new tasks.

I am always happy to discuss research ideas. You can find my papers on the publications page and released work on the projects page.

  1. NeurIPS 2026

    LocalAgent: Collaborative Agentic Verification for Fine-Grained Instance-Level Consistency

    Jiachen Guo, Xinshan Zhu, Boyun Wang, Yanyan Liang, Jun Wan, Feng Lin and Ajian Liu

    The Fortieth Annual Conference on Neural Information Processing Systems (NeurIPS), 2026

    arXiv OpenReview Code

    Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have made rapid progress in general visual understanding, yet they still struggle with fine-grained instance-level consistency verification, where the goal is to determine whether two images depict the exact same physical object under varying viewpoints, illumination, and backgrounds. This task requires both global semantic perception and precise examination of subtle local evidence, since semantically similar objects may differ only in small suspicious regions. We propose LocalAgent, a collaborative agentic framework that integrates the complementary strengths of a generalist MLLM and lightweight specialist verifiers. The MLLM first reasons globally to identify suspicious local regions, which are then delegated to a Local Evidence Verifier (LEV) toolkit for fine-grained appearance and geometric matching. LEV returns structured scalar evidence that the MLLM integrates for the final decision. We further introduce an auxiliary-capped reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT) strategy, in which answer correctness dominates auxiliary tool-use and format signals, enabling the policy to learn when verification is necessary. Experiments show that LocalAgent improves mean accuracy by 4.9% over the strongest CoT-RFT baseline while invoking a verifier on only 12.13% of samples and preserving the general multimodal capabilities of the underlying MLLM.

    Flexible Modal Mixture-of-Experts With Inter-Modal Knowledge Distillation for Face Anti-Spoofing

    Hui Ma, Ajian Liu, Ning Li, Liying Yang, Boyun Wang, Hang Zou, Yuan Zhang, Jing Huang, Zhiqiang Pu, Jun Wan, Zhanchuan Cai, Zhen Lei and Yanyan Liang

    IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, vol. 21, pp. 7215–7228, 2026

    DOI

    Flexible Modal Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS) aims to improve the model’s adaptability to any given deployment modality. The current methods commonly use multiple independent encoders to learn modality-general features for flexible testing, which inevitably involves redundant computation. In this work, we propose a novel Flexible Modal Mixture-of-Experts (FM-MoE) for adaptively learning the representation of multi-modal data in a unified framework, which includes Modal Inter-MoE and Modal Intra-MoE. Modal Inter-MoE learns modality-general and modality-specific features, while Modal Intra-MoE replaces the traditional routing patch token strategy and extracts instance-level spoofing clues from the channel perspective. We further introduce Modal Inter Knowledge Distillation Loss (MI-KDL), which includes a Cross-Modal Loss and a Shared to Multi-Modal Loss (CML and S2MML) to align information across modalities and to transfer shared knowledge to all modalities, preventing the model from excessively optimizing certain single modalities. Extensive experiments demonstrate the flexibility and superiority of our FM-MoE framework over state-of-the-art methods.
